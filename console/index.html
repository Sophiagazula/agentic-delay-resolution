Exceptions

In-transit shipments the model expects to arrive more than 48 hours late, scored for business severity and routed by tier. Tier 1 is handled by the agent; Tier 2 and 3 need a planner.

Activity and overrides

Planner decisions on Tier 2 proposals

Since the start of the snapshot window. A rejection means the planner judged the delay alert a false alarm.

Feedback loop

  • Every approval, rejection and override is stored with the shipment's features and the eventual outcome.
  • Rejection rate by carrier and lane is reviewed with business owners when thresholds are tuned.
  • The classifier is retrained on new outcomes; drift in inputs and in rejection rate triggers an earlier review.

Reading these numbers

  • The manual rule is precise (0.93) but catches only 21% of delays in transit, and late. The model catches 68% at about 40% of planned transit.
  • Precision of 0.58 is acceptable because Tier 2 rejections cost minutes, while a missed delay costs a full investigation after the fact.
  • The most important signal is lateness at the last milestone, followed by the carrier's recent late rate.
  • Before using probabilities as severity weights in production, I would calibrate them (isotonic regression on the validation quarter).

Why the agent is faster

  • Alerts fire at about 40% of planned transit, a median of about 19 days before planned arrival, instead of after a missed date.
  • A quarter of exceptions are resolved in minutes without a planner.
  • Planners spend 30 minutes approving a ready proposal instead of 3 hours investigating.
  • Freed capacity shortens the queue for everything else, which is why gains grow when planners are stretched.

How this console is built

  • Runs the paper's experiment.py unchanged; all 81 published results reproduce exactly.
  • An export step takes the model, test set, severity and tiers and adds per-shipment risk drivers from the forest's decision paths.
  • The policy page replays any threshold on the 12,056 test shipments in the browser.

From the paper

  • 48,000 synthetic shipments, 2024–2025, 12 carriers, 32 lanes, 72% ocean.
  • Random Forest, threshold 0.56, severity S = p × (0.5 inventory + 0.3 SLA + 0.2 value), tiers at 0.20 and 0.40.
  • Agent runs every 4 hours; workflow simulation with planners, schedules and task times.

Added for this console

  • Snapshot moment (Tue 14 Oct 2025, 10:00), shipment IDs and workflow status (approved, rejected, escalated) are illustrative.
  • Tier 2 proposals follow the largest severity driver: inventory → reallocate, SLA → expedite, value → reroute.
  • Risk drivers use tree-path contributions; they explain this model, not causes in the world.
